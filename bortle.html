Understanding the Bortle Scale

Created by John E. Bortle in 2001, this nine-level numeric scale measures the night sky's brightness of a particular location. Class 1 is the darkest sky possible, and Class 9 is a bright inner-city sky.

Class 1-2: Excellent Dark Sky

The Milky Way casts visible shadows on the ground. Thousands of stars are visible to the naked eye. Found only in deep rural deserts and high mountains.

Class 3-4: Rural / Transition

The Milky Way is still clearly visible, but light pollution domes appear on the horizon. This is the minimum requirement for good astrophotography.

Class 5-7: Suburban Sky

The sky is washed out. The Milky Way is invisible or very weak at the zenith (straight up). Only brighter stars and planets can pierce the glow.

Class 8-9: Inner-City Sky

The sky glows orange or white. Constellations are missing stars. You can only see the Moon, planets, and a handful of the absolute brightest stars.
